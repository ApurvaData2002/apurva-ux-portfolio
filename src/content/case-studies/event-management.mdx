    paragraphs:
      - "Organisers create very different events in the same dashboard: a free open session, a free event with limited tickets, or a paid event that also sells products like food."
      - "The dashboard supports seven event types, from Normal to Paid · Ticket + Product, and each one needs different fields."
      - "The wireframes set out the screens. My job was to make each form clear enough that an organiser only fills in what their event needs."

    paragraphs:
      - "My senior designer gave me low-fidelity wireframes for the dashboard and the Create Event form."
      - "I built the form as numbered sections: core information, date and schedule, location, ticketing and pricing, groups and categories, then tickets and products."
      - "Then I edited and rebuilt those sections in each of the seven forms, so every event type has its own complete version."
  - eyebrow: "Key decisions"
    heading: "Choose the event model first"
    layout: stacked
    image: /case-studies/event-management/section-03.png
    alt: "Two parts of the Create Event form: date, schedule and location fields, and the Ticketing & Pricing section asking whether the event needs tickets, products, both or neither, and whether it is free or paid."
    paragraphs:
      - "The Ticketing & Pricing section asks two plain questions: does this event need tickets, products, both or neither, and is it free or paid?"
      - "That choice shapes the rest of the form, so a free open event stays short and a paid event with tickets and products shows every section it needs."
      - "Groups and categories can be picked or created in a pop-over, without leaving the form."
  - eyebrow: "Design decisions"
    heading: "Adding products without losing your place"
    layout: split
    image: /case-studies/event-management/section-04.png

    paragraphs:
      - "Products are added from a picker inside the form, and a new product can be created on the spot."
      - "Each ticket and product sits in its own card with its name, price or spots, description and image, so long forms stay easy to scan."
      - "The same card pattern repeats across all seven forms, so organisers learn it once."

  - eyebrow: "Outcome"
