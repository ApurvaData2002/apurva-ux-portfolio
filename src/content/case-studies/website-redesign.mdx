sections:
  - eyebrow: "Problem"
    heading: "School leaders couldn’t tell what it would cost them"
    layout: stacked
    paragraphs:
      - "BriBooks wanted more eligible schools to finish registering for NYAF. The person deciding is usually a busy principal, often opening the link on a phone from WhatsApp or email."
      - "Before committing, they need five answers: what this is, why their school should join, what it will take, whether to trust it, and what happens next."
      - "The live page never said what taking part would cost, or how much staff time it would need."
  - eyebrow: "Audit"
    heading: "What I found on the live page"
    layout: split
    image: /case-studies/website-redesign/section-02.png
    imageMobile: /case-studies/website-redesign/section-02-wide.png
    alt: "A WCAG 2.2 AA audit of the live NYAF registration page: headline figures such as 38% of text failing contrast, and a table of each issue with its WCAG criterion, the evidence and the fix."
    paragraphs:
      - "There was no analytics to work from, so I listed the five biggest friction points and labelled each one as an observation or a hypothesis."
      - "The form chained State, City and School dropdowns. A school that wasn’t listed got “No options” and couldn’t continue."
      - "On a phone, the Register buttons in the hero and the final section were about 11 screens apart, with nothing in between."
  - eyebrow: "Structure"
    heading: "Answer the questions in the order they’re asked"
    layout: stacked
    image: /case-studies/website-redesign/section-03.png
    alt: "The new landing page as a grey wireframe and as the finished design, with the hero, partner logos and the three-step section From registration to the National Awards."
    paragraphs:
      - "I rebuilt the page around the principal’s five questions. 13 sections became 8: hero, how it works, what it takes, what your school gains, a recognition ladder, proof, FAQ and a final call to action."
      - "The hero states the key facts up front: free for schools, Grades 3–12, and the registration deadline."
      - "How it works became a dated three-step stepper that names who owns each step, so schools can see they only handle the first one."
  - eyebrow: "Registration"
    heading: "A school search that always has a way forward"
    layout: split
    image: /case-studies/website-redesign/section-04.png
    imageMobile: /case-studies/website-redesign/section-04-wide.png
    alt: "Two mobile registration screens: Find your school, with search results and an Already registered badge, and Add your school, for a school that isn’t listed."
    paragraphs:
      - "One search field replaces the three dropdowns. Results show area and city, and an Already registered badge replaces an unexplained green tick."
      - "If a school isn’t listed, “Can’t find your school? Add it” opens a few extra fields instead of a dead end."
      - "The form has three short steps with a step indicator, and sits on its own page so a shared link opens straight on it."
  - eyebrow: "Handoff and measurement"
    heading: "Designing for the principal and the coordinator"
    layout: stacked
    image: /case-studies/website-redesign/section-05.png
    alt: "Three mobile screens that finish registration: About you, with role and contact details, Verify your number, with a 6-digit code by WhatsApp or SMS, and You’re registered, with what happens next and Share with your coordinator."
    paragraphs:
      - "Principals often decide while coordinators fill in the form. That was a hypothesis, suggested by the form’s own role fields."
      - "So Share with your coordinator appears in the hero, the final call to action and the confirmation screen."
      - "I’d measure verified registrations per unique visitor over 7 days and across devices, so a registration finished by a colleague still counts. The first A/B test would check the cost-and-effort message."
  - eyebrow: "Outcome"
    heading: "How I used AI, and what I decided"
    layout: stacked
    paragraphs:
      - "Claude broke down the brief, measured the live page and drafted the audit, journey and plans. I challenged each draft, for example keeping cost above the form dead end because it affects every visitor."
      - "I rejected drafts that invented details, like a real school name in a photo caption, and used labelled placeholders instead of fake testimonials."
