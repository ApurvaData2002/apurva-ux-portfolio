sections:
  - eyebrow: "Problem"
    heading: "Saving a reel is easy. Finding it again is not."
    layout: stacked
    paragraphs:
      - "People save reels to come back to: a Figma trick, a prompt, a recipe. Instagram’s saved folder has no search, no names and no structure, so the thing you saved is effectively gone once the list gets long."
      - "I ran a short survey of nine people who save in order to learn. Seven named finding a save again as the main problem, and Instagram, not YouTube, was where they saved from."
      - "Nobody sorted by length or post date, and nobody deleted a reel once they had watched it. That removed two features I had planned, and added one I had not: search by keyword."
  - eyebrow: "Research"
    heading: "Twenty-nine apps, and the gap none of them filled"
    layout: stacked
    paragraphs:
      - "I screened 29 saver apps, shortlisted 10 and tested four by hand on my own phone."
      - "Albo plays reels in the app with no Instagram sign-in at all, which points to re-hosted copies. Quiki needs a sign-up and puts Instagram behind a paywall. Several of the rest were downloaders, or had gone from the Play Store in India."
      - "The gap was an app that keeps your own saves, in folders you name, with search, and no feed to fall back into."
  - eyebrow: "Constraints"
    heading: "What the platforms would and would not allow"
    layout: split
    image: /case-studies/laterloft/section-03.png
    imageMobile: /case-studies/laterloft/section-03-wide.png
    alt: "Two phone screens: an Instagram reel with the Android share sheet open on Sharing link, and Laterloft’s Save to a category sheet with the title filled in from YouTube, category chips and suggested keywords."
    paragraphs:
      - "This product was shaped more by terms of service than by taste. Instagram’s terms ban automated collection and forbid modifying its embed. Meta’s oEmbed forbids keeping anything it returns. Cover images expire after about five days, so a copy is saved to the phone instead."
      - "An Instagram reel only plays inside the app if you are already signed in to Instagram in that web view. So connecting Instagram became optional rather than required: without it a reel opens in Instagram, with it the reel plays in Laterloft."
      - "YouTube’s API wants saved details refreshed every 30 days and an age confirmation before first use. Nothing of ours is ever drawn on top of a playing embed."
  - eyebrow: "Decisions"
    heading: "The card and the viewer, each picked from four"
    layout: stacked
    image: /case-studies/laterloft/section-04.png
    alt: "Two phone screens: a category called Claude Shorts holding a grid of saved reels with the creator’s name on each cover, and the viewer with a reel playing above its title, creator, category and keyword chips."
    paragraphs:
      - "I drew four versions of the reel card and four of the viewer, then chose against the persona rather than by preference."
      - "The card puts the creator’s name on the cover, because someone saving to learn recognises a reel by its thumbnail and who made it, not by a title they never wrote. Platform and length moved to a quieter line underneath."
      - "The viewer treats a reel as a filed note: the video, then the title, the creator, the category and the keywords, all editable where they sit. Search ranks by best match rather than newest, because the point is to find one particular thing."
  - eyebrow: "Craft"
    heading: "Warm Attic, in light and dark"
    layout: split
    image: /case-studies/laterloft/section-05.png
    imageMobile: /case-studies/laterloft/section-05-wide.png
    alt: "The Laterloft library in light mode and dark mode: a search field, a Recently saved row of reel covers, and a grid of categories with cover images and reel counts."
    paragraphs:
      - "Three brand directions went on a board. Warm Attic won: Fraunces and Nunito Sans, a forest green and a honey."
      - "It became a design system of 90 colour tokens and 19 components, light and dark. A script checks every colour pairing the components actually use against WCAG AA in both themes, and it runs on every change."
      - "The rule I kept returning to: never colour alone. Status is carried by an icon and words as well, so it survives a colourblind reader and a dimmed screen."
  - eyebrow: "Accessibility"
    heading: "Motion you can switch off, and a layout for either hand"
    layout: stacked
    paragraphs:
      - "Motion arrived in two tiers, 22 animations in all, each with a reduced-motion fallback. The app also carries its own Remove animations switch that takes effect immediately, for the many people who do not know Android has the setting."
      - "Snackbars last three seconds, five with an action, and longer if Android’s Time to take action setting asks for it. A deleted cover waits over two minutes before it is really gone, so Undo always has something to undo."
      - "A left-handed layout moves the add button and the row actions across. The top bar deliberately stays put: Back should never move, because that is the control you reach for without looking."
  - eyebrow: "Where it stands"
    heading: "Built, not yet released"
    layout: stacked
    paragraphs:
      - "Version 1.0 is finished and running on my phone. The library lives on the device, there is a CSV export, and the privacy policy is written for Indian law."
      - "It is not on the Play Store yet, and the code stays in a private repo until the waitlist is big enough to be worth a closed test: Google wants twelve testers for fourteen days before a personal account can publish."
      - "What I left out is written down rather than forgotten: a loop button only YouTube can support, keyword suggestions read from covers on the device, and an iPhone version."
